Subject: Would you count some cells for me? About 30 minutes, anonymous, no
         expertise needed

Hello,

I am writing to ask whether you would be willing to help with a study I am
running at the University of Plymouth. Please read to the end before deciding —
and please feel free to ignore this if it does not appeal. I will not know who
took part and who did not, and it makes no difference to me either way.

WHAT IT IS ABOUT

When researchers want to know how many cells in a piece of tissue are alive and
how many are dead, somebody usually counts them by eye in microscope images.
That human count is then treated as the correct answer, and computer programs
written to do the job automatically get judged against it.

We have reason to think that answer is shakier than it looks. Two experienced
colleagues separately counted the same images and came to very different
conclusions — one thought about 12% of the cells were alive, the other about 41%.
Identical pictures.

So I am asking a simple question: how consistently do people actually count cells
in these images? To answer it I need a group of people to each count the same
kind of images independently.

WHAT YOU WOULD DO

Open a link, read a short page explaining how to count, and then click on the
cells you can see in a series of small microscope images, saying whether each one
looks alive, dead, or you are not sure.

  - It takes about 20 to 40 minutes.
  - It works in any web browser, on a computer, tablet or phone. Nothing to
    install.
  - You need no expertise at all. How well people without a microscopy
    background do this is part of what I am measuring.
  - You can stop at any point and pick up later.
  - It is available in English and German.

WHAT I WOULD KNOW ABOUT YOU

Nothing. The study is anonymous by design. The web page gives you a random code
such as "amber-larch-63" and that is the only label on your answers. I do not ask
for your name, your email address, or anything else. There is no list anywhere
linking codes to people, so I could not work out who you are even if I wanted to.

That does mean one thing worth knowing in advance: if you later want your data
deleted, you have to quote that code, because it is the only way to find your
answers. The page asks you to write it down before you start.

BEING STRAIGHT WITH YOU

  - There is no payment, voucher or prize draw. It is unpaid.
  - There is no personal benefit to you.
  - There is a short practice round, and it is possible not to pass it first
    time. You can retry as often as you like, nothing happens if you do not
    pass, and because the study is anonymous nobody — including me — can tell
    who that was.
  - Saying no has no consequence whatsoever, and you do not need to give a
    reason. I would genuinely rather you declined than felt obliged.

I am not able to recruit anyone I supervise, teach, assess or line manage, so if
that describes our working relationship, please disregard this — it is not that
your help would be unwelcome, but that I should not be asking.

WORUM ES GEHT

Wenn man wissen will, wie viele Zellen in einem Gewebe leben und wie viele tot
sind, zählt sie üblicherweise jemand mit dem Auge in Mikroskopbildern aus. Dieses
menschliche Ergebnis gilt dann als die richtige Antwort — und daran werden
Computerprogramme gemessen, die diese Aufgabe automatisch erledigen sollen.

Wir haben Grund zu der Annahme, dass diese Antwort weniger belastbar ist, als sie
wirkt. Zwei erfahrene Kolleginnen haben dieselben Bilder unabhängig voneinander
ausgezählt und kamen zu sehr unterschiedlichen Ergebnissen: die eine auf etwa 12%
lebende Zellen, die andere auf etwa 41%. Bei identischen Bildern.

Deshalb stelle ich eine einfache Frage: Wie einheitlich zählen Menschen Zellen in
solchen Bildern tatsächlich? Dafür brauche ich mehrere Personen, die dieselbe Art
von Bildern unabhängig voneinander auszählen.

WAS SIE TUN WÜRDEN

Einen Link öffnen, eine kurze Anleitung lesen und dann in mehreren kleinen
Mikroskopbildern die erkennbaren Zellen anklicken und jeweils angeben, ob sie
lebend, tot oder unsicher erscheinen.

  - Das dauert etwa 20 bis 40 Minuten.
  - Es läuft in jedem Webbrowser, auf Computer, Tablet oder Handy. Es wird
    nichts installiert.
  - Sie brauchen keinerlei Vorkenntnisse. Wie gut Menschen ohne
    Mikroskopie-Hintergrund das können, ist Teil dessen, was ich messe.
  - Sie können jederzeit aufhören und später weitermachen.

NOTES — NOT PART OF THE EMAIL
================================================================================

Why it is worded this way, in case PIERC asks about B11 (fair and non-coercive
recruitment):

  - It states twice that declining has no consequence, and once that a refusal
    is genuinely preferred over a reluctant yes.
  - It excludes supervisees, students, appraisees and direct reports in the body
    of the email itself, so the exclusion is visible to the recipient rather
    than only asserted on the form.
  - It says plainly there is no payment and no personal benefit, rather than
    implying a benefit that does not exist.
  - It discloses the practice round and the possibility of not passing before
    anyone commits, rather than at the point they meet it.
  - It gives the time cost honestly as a range, not a best case.
  - It contains no deadline, no reminder-chasing language, and no appeal to
    obligation or reciprocity.

Two practical points:

  - Send it individually or BCC. A visible group recruitment email would let
    recipients see who else was asked, which weakens the anonymity the study
    depends on.
  - Do NOT use per-person access keys for the participant round. They exist for
    the named expert panel, where the key identifies the counter on purpose.
    Attaching one to a participant would tie a real person to their answers and
    destroy the anonymity this whole design rests on.
