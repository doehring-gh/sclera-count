> ### Read this first — the template has been adapted, deliberately
>
> The standard template opens with **"Full name of participant"**, an
> **"Identification number for this study"**, and closes with a **signature**.
>
> Collecting any of those would create exactly the identifiable record this study
> is designed not to hold. A signed form carrying a name, stored alongside the
> data, would convert genuinely anonymous data into personal data — and would mean
> the University held a name-to-answers link that currently does not exist
> anywhere.
>
> **So consent is taken as an on-screen confirmation instead**, presented as the
> first screen of the task, with the same statements the paper form would carry.
> The participant cannot begin until they have actively confirmed each one. What
> is recorded is that consent was given, with a timestamp and the participant's
> self-generated code — not who gave it.
>
> This is the standard and appropriate arrangement for anonymous online research,
> but it is a departure from the template and the committee should see it as a
> considered decision. The wording below is written to be read on screen.
>
> **Both versions are given:** the on-screen version (what participants actually
> see) and a paper version (should the committee require the template form to be
> submitted in its usual shape).

## Notes for you — not for participants

**A separate consent route is needed for the expert reference panel.** They are
named, not anonymous, and their individual agreement with the consensus is
reported. Their consent is by email reply to a written invitation covering: what
the annotation involves, that their individual agreement with the group will be
reported, whether they wish to be named or reported as "Expert A, B, C…", and the
offer of acknowledgement or co-authorship. If PIERC wants that documented
formally, it needs its own short PIS and ICF at version `V1.0_experts` — say the
word and I will draft them.

**One thing to check with the committee.** Some committees dislike consent that
cannot be re-presented to a named individual. If PIERC pushes back on the
on-screen approach, the fallback is a signed form held entirely separately from
the data, with no shared identifier — but that reintroduces a name into the study
and would need the PIS confidentiality section rewritten. It is worth defending
the anonymous version first: it is genuinely the more protective design.

(must match the PIS exactly).
