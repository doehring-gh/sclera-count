## Hazard table

### Participant-facing (C01)

**1. Display screen use — eye strain, fatigue, postural discomfort**

- *Who might be harmed and how:* Participants, from approximately 20–40 minutes of
  close visual attention to a screen. Aggravated by small or dim image detail.
- *Existing controls:* Task is explicitly designed to be interruptible — progress
  saves continuously and participants can stop and resume at any point, so no
  unbroken session is required. Images can be zoomed and their brightness and
  contrast adjusted by the participant. The Participant Information Sheet advises
  taking breaks as with any screen work. Participants choose their own device,
  environment and timing.
- **L = 2 (Unlikely) · S = 1 (Insignificant) · RS = 2 — Low**
- *Further action:* None. Accept.
- **Target: L = 2 · S = 1 · RS = 2**

**2. Mild frustration or discouragement from not passing the practice round**

- *Who might be harmed and how:* Participants, psychologically and only mildly. The
  study requires a set level of agreement with a reference before counts are
  included, so it is possible not to pass at the first attempt, which some people
  may find dispiriting.
- *Existing controls:* Unlimited attempts, with no penalty for any number. The
  threshold is derived from what the expert reference panel themselves achieved
  rather than set arbitrarily, so participants are never asked to outperform the
  people defining the standard. Feedback is worded as calibration, not assessment.
  Participation is anonymous, so no individual's performance is known to the
  research team or to anyone else, and results are never reported per individual.
  The Participant Information Sheet states plainly, before consent, that not
  passing is possible and carries no consequence. Participants may stop at any
  point.
- **L = 2 (Unlikely) · S = 1 (Insignificant) · RS = 2 — Low**
- *Further action:* None. The residual risk is below that ordinarily encountered
  in daily life. Accept.
- **Target: L = 2 · S = 1 · RS = 2**

**3. Re-identification of a participant by inference from recorded metadata**

- *Who might be harmed and how:* Participants, through loss of anonymity. No
  identifying data is collected, but the application records time spent per image,
  display settings chosen, interface language, and timestamps. Within a small pool
  of participants, an unusual combination — for example a session conducted
  overnight in German — could in principle be attributed by someone who knew who
  had been invited.
- *Existing controls:* No name, email address or other identifier is collected at
  any point; the application issues a randomly generated code which the
  participant alone holds, and no code-to-identity record exists anywhere.
  Per-participant metadata will not be reported or published at that granularity,
  and timestamps will be coarsened before any dataset is shared. Recruitment is by
  open invitation rather than a fixed, knowable list.
- **L = 2 (Unlikely) · S = 2 (Minor) · RS = 4 — Low**
- *Further action:* Confirm at the point of deposit that the published dataset
  contains no per-participant timing or timestamp at finer than daily resolution.
- **Target: L = 1 · S = 2 · RS = 2**

**4. Participant loses their code and cannot withdraw their data**

- *Who might be harmed and how:* Participants, through loss of control over their
  data. Withdrawal depends entirely on the participant quoting their code, because
  no link between code and person exists.
- *Existing controls:* The code is displayed prominently at the start; the task
  cannot begin until the participant confirms they have recorded it; generating a
  new code clears that confirmation so it cannot be bypassed accidentally; and the
  code is shown again on the final screen. The consequence of losing it is stated
  explicitly in both the Participant Information Sheet and the consent
  confirmation, before any data is collected.
- **L = 3 (Possible) · S = 1 (Insignificant) · RS = 3 — Low**
- *Further action:* None. This is an inherent and disclosed consequence of
  anonymity, and the alternative — holding a name-to-code key — would be a greater
  intrusion. Accept.
- **Target: L = 3 · S = 1 · RS = 3**

**5. Loss of submitted data, wasting participant time**

- *Who might be harmed and how:* Participants, through their contribution being
  lost and their time wasted.
- *Existing controls:* Work is saved continuously in the participant's own browser
  as they go, so an interruption loses nothing. On completion the participant
  downloads their results file and returns it to the researcher, and the
  application confirms on screen that the download has been produced. Received
  files are stored immediately in University OneDrive for Business, which is
  institutionally backed up.
- **L = 2 (Unlikely) · S = 2 (Minor) · RS = 4 — Low**
- *Further action:* Acknowledge receipt of each returned file, so a participant
  knows their contribution arrived.
- **Target: L = 1 · S = 2 · RS = 2**

### Researcher-facing (D01)

**6. Display screen equipment use by the research team**

- *Who might be harmed and how:* The research team, from extended computer work
  during software development, image processing and analysis — eye strain,
  postural discomfort, repetitive strain.
- *Existing controls:* Work is office- or home-based on University-provided
  equipment, within normal working hours. Standard University DSE assessment
  completed, workstation set up accordingly, and regular breaks taken in line with
  the University Health, Safety and Wellbeing Policy.
- **L = 2 (Unlikely) · S = 2 (Minor) · RS = 4 — Low**
- *Further action:* None. Accept.
- **Target: L = 2 · S = 2 · RS = 4**

**7. Lone working**

- *Who might be harmed and how:* Not applicable. All research activity is
  computer-based and conducted in normal University or home working environments.
  There is no fieldwork, no laboratory work under this application, no travel, and
  no in-person contact with participants at any point.
- *Existing controls:* Not applicable. No aspect of this study requires working
  alone in an isolated or hazardous setting.
- **L = 1 (Very unlikely) · S = 1 (Insignificant) · RS = 1 — Low**
- *Further action:* None.
- **Target: L = 1 · S = 1 · RS = 1**

**8. Unauthorised or spurious submissions corrupting the dataset**

- *Who might be harmed and how:* The research team and the integrity of the
  research, if someone other than an invited participant submitted data, or if a
  participant submitted more than once.
- *Existing controls:* Participants receive an individually issued link. Returned
  files are matched on the participant's self-generated code, so a resubmission
  replaces rather than duplicates an earlier one. No payment or incentive is
  offered, removing the motive for fraudulent participation. The task requires
  interpreting images, so automated submission is impractical. Quality is
  monitored through data recorded automatically — time per image, practice
  attempts, and agreement on shared anchor images — any of which would identify an
  implausible submission. All data is held in University OneDrive under the PI's
  control; no third-party platform is used.
- **L = 2 (Unlikely) · S = 2 (Minor) · RS = 4 — Low**
- *Further action:* Reconcile the number of files received against the number of
  invitations issued at the close of collection.
- **Target: L = 1 · S = 2 · RS = 2**
